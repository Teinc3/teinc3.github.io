## Daily Agenda
*Date: October 15, 2026*

Having thought about it I realised that the problem doesn't that "I don't have
enough projects" or "I don't have enough experience". In fact, it seems to me
that I have a considerably deeper share of experience than virtually anyone else
in my cohort. The problem seems to be entirely about how (the way) my experience
is framed instead.

We can think about a hackathon project as an example: you see a vibecoded
dashboard full of shiny metrics, which gives you the feeling that "damn, this
project must be good!" without even going through it's internals at all.
Chances are it's an extremely thin LLM wrapper, with no advanced architecture.
Contrast that to my work, which you will certainly consider complex, doesn't
necessarily have the best presentation to it and doesn't feel that impressive.
I don't despise data plumbing, simultaneously also having done a lot of it, but
you can't really "present" it as a glamorous thing on its own without a good UI.

So instead of spamming more projects, a good idea would be to work on
"beautifying" my existing projects as much as possible. As well as to attract a
sizeable stakeholder/audience via marketing (which takes time) and focus on 
metrics/impact more during the framing, as all of them have the potential of
(or is already) attracing a significant amount of users.

I also determined that the "Execution velocity" demanded by interviews seems
rather different from that you get by doing projects. The former requires a
completely different skill set which you can only acquire by doing interview
practice. Well, I have 10 months. A mock decomp every week gives 40 decomps.
That should definitely be enough to plug this specific hole.
